# Example KetenScan — Scenario 1: VVT / VOS renewal

Status: **APPROVED FOR GATE B DEVELOPMENT**
Priority: **P1**
Governance issue: #2

## Purpose

Create a synthetic but operationally credible KetenScan that proves what ZorgTechAdvies actually delivers before a care organisation starts a PvE, supplier selection or implementation programme.

The asset must show how an apparently technical replacement question is reframed into a governable care-critical chain assignment.

## Synthetic organisation

Use a clearly fictitious medium-sized Dutch VVT organisation with approximately:

- 8 intramural locations;
- ~900 clients;
- PG, somatics and ELV;
- a nurse-call / care-domotics estate that has grown over several years;
- meaningful differences between locations in configuration, infrastructure and working practice.

Do not mimic an identifiable real organisation. Names, dates, incidents and quantities beyond the approved high-level frame are synthetic.

## Visible trigger

The incumbent supplier has announced lifecycle/end-of-life pressure on parts of the existing nurse-call environment. At the same time there are increasing incidents, alarm load and complaints about reachability. ICT is inclined to start a requirements specification and supplier selection.

Initial client question:

> Can you help us determine which new nurse-call system we need?

## Reframed assignment

There is not yet a shared definition of what actually needs replacement or redesign. Care teams mainly experience excessive alerts; ICT sees ageing technology and fragmented infrastructure; real estate has renovation plans; care wants more mobile support; management wants standardisation; some local arrangements work well.

Central problem statement:

> How can the organisation renew its care-critical alarm and communication chain in a future-proof way without starting a technical replacement programme before the required functions, responsibilities, infrastructure and working processes are sufficiently clear?

## Mandatory analytical tensions

- safety vs alarm load;
- standardisation vs local workability;
- lifecycle urgency vs careful problem definition;
- existing infrastructure vs desired functionality;
- technology investment vs solving process/governance problems;
- local preference vs chain interest where relevant.

## Required draft structure

1. Synthetic-case disclaimer and purpose.
2. Management summary.
3. Context, trigger and original question.
4. Reframing: why "replace the VOS" is not yet a sufficient assignment.
5. Synthetic fact base, assumptions and unknowns — explicitly separated.
6. Stakeholder perspectives: care, ICT, facilities/real estate, management, functional/application management and project/innovation where relevant.
7. Care-critical chain analysis using only relevant domains from the nine-domain lens.
8. Current-state problem map / causal chain.
9. Risks and dependencies ranked by decision relevance and severity.
10. Three materially different strategic scenarios with explicit trade-offs.
11. Recommendation logic and preferred direction.
12. What should deliberately not be decided yet.
13. Governance, ownership and decision-right implications.
14. Executable next step and 30/60/90-day route.
15. Compact decision memo for management/board.
16. Method/instrument appendices only where they demonstrate value.

## Scenario quality rule

The three strategic scenarios must be genuinely different choices, not good/better/best packaging. At least one scenario should preserve more of the existing environment, one should pursue a controlled broader renewal, and one may explore a more fundamental chain redesign. Their feasibility and desirability must be argued from function, risk, manageability and organisational readiness — never from named vendors.

## Evidence-state discipline

Keep separate:

- synthetic facts;
- assumptions;
- observed/constructed symptoms;
- analysis/inference;
- risks;
- recommendation;
- decisions still required from the client.

Do not manufacture certainty to make the document look decisive.

## Boundaries

This deliverable is **not**:

- a full PvE;
- a procurement document;
- a supplier ranking;
- a detailed technical architecture;
- a final legal/privacy/security opinion;
- an implementation plan beyond a bounded executable next step.
